Tasks, families and capabilities#

A task is the part of a config that says what the trials were for. Given the stimuli, it decides three things: each trial’s category, the correct response, and how the response is scored, as an Outcome (correct, error, no response, abort or unscored). The library knows no experiment; the meaning of a trial lives in the task, and nothing else second-guesses it.

Families#

A family is defined by what a response is.

  • Binary: the response is 0 or 1. In TwoAFC both responses are active choices; in GoNoGo 0 means withholding, so a blank response that is not an abort is scored as a withhold rather than lost.

  • Continuous: the response is a value on the stimulus scale. Reproduction counts a response as correct within a tolerance of the stimulus.

Each family has its own statistics, readouts and plots and a descriptive model, and IdealObserver serves both as the normative model: it gives the more probable category, or the posterior mean of the stimulus. Loading, scoring, selection, pooling, contrasts, resampling and synthetic data work the same way for every family. A K-choice family is planned (see the roadmap).

Capabilities#

What a task provides is a set of capability names, and statistics, readouts and models declare the capabilities they need. Asking for one that the task cannot support raises a RequirementError that names what is missing, what the task has and, where there is one, the statistic to use instead. Behaviour depends on a task’s capabilities and response space, never on its class, so a task of your own (see Extending) gets every statistic its capabilities allow.
